# goals summary

one line per goal, grouped by life area. the detail lives in [goals/](goals/), one note per
goal. status, criteria, and milestones are mirrored in the tracker by id.

| id | goal | life area | status | outcome | next review | note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| _none recorded_ | | | | | | |

## the hierarchy

```text
life area
└── goal
    ├── success criteria
    ├── milestones
    │   └── projects or workstreams
    │       └── next actions and checklists
    └── recurring habits or routines
```

a goal may need only a checklist. another may need three projects and two habits. build the
smallest structure that makes the next step obvious.

## every active goal has

- a clear outcome and a definition of done
- a small number of measurable success criteria
- the next milestone
- at least one concrete next action, when action is currently possible
- any recurring habits that support it
- a review date or cadence
- a status: `not_started` · `active` · `blocked` · `paused` · `completed` · `abandoned`
- a recorded reason whenever it changes, pauses, or is abandoned

## what a goal note captures

outcome, baseline, target, rationale, constraints, deadline, measurement method, plan, review
cadence, progress, and the adjustment trigger — the condition that means the plan should
change rather than be pushed harder.

## no fake precision

some goals are binary. some are milestone-based. some carry a percentage. some can only be
reviewed qualitatively. use the one that fits.

progress comes from success criteria or completed milestones. it never comes from counting
tasks, hours, or elapsed time. "60% of the time has passed" is not 60% progress, and a goal
with four criteria and one met is 1/4 — stated as 1/4, not smoothed into a curve.

## goals are not the same as

- **projects** — a project is work with an end; a goal is an outcome.
- **milestones** — checkpoints inside a goal.
- **tasks** — single actions.
- **habits** — recurring behavior that supports a goal but never *is* the goal, and whose
  completion is never claimed to have caused the outcome.
